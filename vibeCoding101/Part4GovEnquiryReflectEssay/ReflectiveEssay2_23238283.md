## Introduction

This essay reflects on my experience drafting a government enquiry for the flu vaccination project, focusing on data access, AI collaboration, and the evolution of my workflow.

## Main Body

Reviewing the planGovEnquiries.md, I found that much health data is open, but key details require formal requests. This led me to shift from improvisational queries to a “prompts as code” approach, where instructions are versioned, reviewed, and reused. Sending file paths to the AI agent reduces copy/paste errors and saves time. It also improves reviewability, as teammates can open PRs, comment, and legal teams can sign off once. The AI agent helped clarify missing data and structure my requests. Writing instruction files is much like drafting government enquiries: clarity, specificity, structure, and referencing authority are essential. One challenge is capturing tacit knowledge—much is unwritten. Mitigating this with examples, rationales, and glossaries makes instructions more accessible.

## Conclusion

This experience deepened my understanding of data-driven policymaking and government data access. I am now better prepared to request data and use AI support for future research.
